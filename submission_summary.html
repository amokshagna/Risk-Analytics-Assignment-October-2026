10 Foundational Differences: RBI ICAAP Guidelines vs. Basel III Strict 2-Page Research Paper
# Dimension Basel III Global Standards RBI ICAAP Guidelines Macroprudential Rationale
1 Minimum CET1 Ratio 4.5% of RWAs 5.5% of RWAs (+100 bps) Loss-absorption against emerging market cyclicality.
2 Minimum Tier 1 Capital 6.0% of RWAs 7.0% of RWAs (+100 bps) Ensures high-quality going-concern equity base.
3 Minimum Total Capital (CRAR) 8.0% of RWAs 9.0% of RWAs (11.5% with CCB) Pillar 1 super-equivalent baseline for Indian banks.
4 Capital Conservation Buffer 2.5% phased 2016-19 2.5% with COVID-19 deferrals Preserved credit delivery during pandemic shock.
5 Countercyclical Buffer (CCCB) 0% to 2.5% activated on Credit/GDP gap Maintained at 0% (targeted hikes used) Avoided penalizing structural financial deepening.
6 Sovereign Debt Risk Weights Rating-linked (non-zero for sub-investment) 0% on Central & State (SDL) Debt Maintains seamless sovereign borrowing & SLR pre-emption.
7 Liquidity Coverage (LCR) & SLR HQLA strictly cash & central bank reserves Carves out SLR via FALLCR & MSF Harmonizes legacy 18% SLR with Basel LCR norms.
8 Loan Provisioning Model IFRS 9 Expected Credit Loss (3 stages) Incurred Loss with 90-day formulaic norms Supervisory formula prevents subjective under-provisioning.
9 IRRBB Capital Treatment Pillar 2 supervisory outlier test (+/-200 bps) Mandatory Pillar 2 capital add-on under ICAAP Protects heavy fixed-rate government bond holdings.
10 D-SIB Surcharges & Board Oversight National discretion; principle-based Explicit surcharges: SBI (+0.8%), HDFC (+0.4%) Enforces board accountability and mitigates moral hazard.
